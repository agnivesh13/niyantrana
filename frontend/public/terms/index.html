1. What the service does

It estimates the likelihood of four metabolic conditions — fatty liver, raised blood pressure, raised blood sugar and type-2 diabetes — from measurements and daily habits you record, and shows how those estimates move over time. Every number is labelled with where it came from, and the app reports an error rather than a guess when it cannot produce a real estimate.

2. What it does not do

  • It does not diagnose. A high score is a prompt to get tested, not a finding.
  • It does not replace a blood test, a blood-pressure cuff, or a clinician.
  • It is not monitored. Nobody is watching your scores, and nothing in this app will alert a doctor or an emergency service on your behalf.

3. Accuracy, stated honestly

The risk models were trained on NHANES 2013–2018 and evaluated on a held-out cycle. On that holdout they reach AUROC 0.80 for hypertension, 0.80 for raised blood sugar, 0.80 for type-2 diabetes and 0.96 for fatty liver — and that last figure is close to a tautology, because BMI and waist circumference alone reach 0.955.

Two limitations follow from that and apply to every score you see. The models are calibrated on a United States population, so they are systematically off for South Asian thresholds; and high predicted probabilities are overconfident. Treat the output as a rough signal, not a measurement.
