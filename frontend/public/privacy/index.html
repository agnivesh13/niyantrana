Niyantrana

Privacy Policy

Last updated 13 September 2026

Niyantrana is a metabolic-health screening tool operated by an individual developer, Swami Agnivesh, as a personal and educational project. It estimates risk of fatty liver, raised blood pressure, raised blood sugar and type-2 diabetes from the things you record: your basic body measurements, the meals you log, and day-level activity, sleep and heart-rate data from a wearable device.

Niyantrana is not a medical device and does not diagnose anything. Every score it produces is a screening estimate. Confirm any concern with a clinician and a real blood test.

1. Who is responsible for your data

This app is run by one person, not a company. Questions, data access requests and deletion requests all go to the same address: agniveshshaga@gmail.com.

2. What the app collects

Only what a risk estimate actually needs. There is no advertising, no analytics or tracking script of any kind, and no third-party cookie in this application.

DataWhy it is collected
Account — your email address; a bcrypt hash of your password if you use one; your Google account identifier if you sign in with Google To create your account and keep your records separate from anyone else's. If you sign in with Google, we store only your email address and Google's opaque account identifier — not your name or profile picture.
Profile — age, sex, height, weight, waist circumference, family history of diabetes or liver disease, alcohol intake, smoking status These are the inputs of the risk models. Waist and BMI in particular drive the fatty-liver estimate.
Meals you log — the food chosen, servings, the resulting macronutrients, and the time Daily energy, fat, carbohydrate, protein, fibre and sugar totals are features the models use.
Measurements you enter — blood pressure, HbA1c, triglycerides, GGT, fasting glucose, weight, waist A real measured value replaces the model's estimate for that biomarker, which makes your assessment more accurate than a prediction.
Day-level wearable data — steps, active minutes, sleep duration, sleep efficiency, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, and which source each day came from The models score a 14-day window of this data, and the risk trend is produced by scoring successive windows of your own history.
Assessments produced — estimated biomarkers, risk scores and bands, and where each number came from Stored so your risk trend over time can be shown.
Google Health access tokens — if you connect Google Health To read the wearable data above on your behalf. Stored so you do not have to re-authorise on every sync.
A session cookie To keep you signed in. It is HttpOnly, so page scripts cannot read it, and it is not used for tracking.

3. Data obtained from Google APIs

Sign in with Google

If you use the Google button, Niyantrana requests the openid, email and profile scopes. It reads your email address and Google account identifier to create or find your account, and stores only those two things. The credential Google issues is verified on our server against Google's public keys and then discarded.

Google Health API

If — and only if — you choose to connect Google Health, Niyantrana requests read-only access to your activity and fitness, sleep, and health-metrics data. It reads exactly six values per day: steps, active minutes, sleep duration, sleep efficiency, daily resting heart rate and daily heart-rate variability. It requests no write access, and it does not read your location, ECG, nutrition logs or reproductive-health data.

Those daily values are stored on your account and used for one purpose: producing the risk scores and the risk trend that the app shows you. You can disconnect at any time from the app's onboarding screen, or revoke access directly at your Google account permissions page. Disconnecting stops all further reading immediately.

Limited Use commitment

Niyantrana's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Concretely: data from Google APIs is used only to provide the risk-screening features you can see in the app; it is never used for advertising; it is never sold or transferred to anyone for their own purposes; and no human reads it, including the developer.

4. Who your data is shared with

Your data is never sold, rented, or shared for advertising. It reaches only the services required to run the app:

  • MongoDB Atlas — the database that stores your account and records. Encrypted in transit and at rest.
  • Render — hosts the application server and the machine-learning service, both operated by the developer.
  • Cloudflare — serves the web pages themselves.
  • Google Gemini API — if you use the in-app assistant, the message you type is sent to Google's Gemini API together with a short summary of your most recent assessment, so the answer can refer to your own numbers. If you never open the assistant, nothing is sent. Your email address and account identifier are never included.

The risk models themselves run on the developer's own server. Your measurements are not sent to any third-party machine-learning provider.

5. Where your data is processed

On servers operated by the providers above, which may be located outside India, including in the United States and the European Union.

6. How long it is kept, and how to delete it

Your records are kept for as long as your account exists, because the risk trend is built from your own history and deleting the history removes the feature.

  • Disconnect Google Health at any time in the app. Access stops immediately and the stored tokens are deleted. Wearable days already imported are kept, because they are your own history — ask for deletion if you want them gone too.
  • Delete everything by emailing agniveshshaga@gmail.com from your account's email address. Your account and all associated records — profile, meals, measurements, wearable days, assessments and tokens — are deleted within 30 days, and normally much sooner.
  • Ask for a copy of your data at the same address.

7. Security

  • All traffic is served over HTTPS.
  • Passwords are stored only as bcrypt hashes. If you sign in with Google there is no password at all.
  • Google Health tokens and password hashes are excluded from database queries by default, so ordinary application code cannot read them.
  • The session cookie is HttpOnly and Secure.
  • No API key or secret is included in the code that runs in your browser.

This is a personal project run by one developer, not an accredited medical service. It is not certified under HIPAA or any equivalent framework. Please decide what you record here with that in mind.

8. Children

Niyantrana is not intended for anyone under 18, and the risk models were fitted on adults only. Accounts are not knowingly created for children.

9. A limitation worth stating plainly

The risk models were trained on NHANES 2013–2018, a United States population. South Asians develop metabolic disease at lower BMI and waist thresholds, so scores are systematically off for the population this app was built for. This is documented in the project's own results write-up rather than hidden.
